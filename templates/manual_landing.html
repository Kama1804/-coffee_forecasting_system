2. Detailed End-to-End System Pipeline Flow

Follow this chronological flow to import raw cashier registers, verify ingredient parameters, and view decision-support charts.

Exporting & Uploading Raw POS CSV Data

Open your outlet's point-of-sale console and export the historical transactions as a CSV file. Go to the Data Ingestion page in your sidebar and drag the file into the secure drop zone.

The platform scans the file headers against the 17-column raw layout schema. If any expected headers are missing, the interface flags an immediate error, preventing bad columns from reaching the warehouse database.

Formatting, Weather Integration, & Data Enrichment

Once the CSV file is verified, the system processes it. The pipeline cleans transaction times into normalized attributes like hour, day, and month strings.

To account for weather-driven sales, the engine queries localized meteorological logs. Using coordinate bounding boxes, it maps transactions to historical weather states (Sunny, Cloudy, Raining, or Thunderstorm).

Engine: Data Cleansing & Ingestion Enrichment: Weather state & temperature tracking Weather API: Open-Meteo & OpenWeatherMap
3
Phase 3: Recipe Verification
Recipe Integrity & Material Audits

The system matches menu items with defined material weights (such as coffee beans, milk, chocolate, and cup sizes) in the recipe registry. If a new menu item has no recipe parameters, the system prompts the owner.

Maintaining recipes allows the system to convert daily beverage forecast volumes into exact purchase-order ingredient amounts.

Registry: Recipe Database Metrics: Coffee beans, milk, chocolate, and cup sizes

Meta Prophet Time-Series Predictions

Now, navigate to the AI Forecast view. This triggers the machine learning engine to train a Facebook Prophet multiplicative regression model.

The engine combines historical sales, localized weather predictions (via a 3-attempt retry loop), and public holiday multipliers to generate a 5-day forecast. It also translates expected beverage sales into precise ingredient lists (e.g., total grams of coffee beans or milliliters of milk needed).

Engine: AI Forecasting Module Model: Prophet regression Features: Malaysia public holidays & 5-day weather forecasts

Dashboard Metrics & Calculations
Metric: Gross Sales vs. Retained Cash (Net Sales)

Evaluates revenue retention by tracking total baseline menu earnings (Gross Sales), promotional losses, and actual net cash collected into your store account.

$$\text{Net Sales (Retained Cash)} = \text{Gross Sales} - \text{Total Promotional Discounts}$$ $$\text{Revenue Retention Rate (\%)} = \left( \frac{\text{Net Sales (Retained Cash)}}{\text{Gross Sales}} \right) \times 100$$
Example & Business Guidance:

If your store generates RM 5,000.00 in Gross Sales (at standard menu prices), but gave away RM 750.00 in promotional discounts:
$$\text{Net Sales (Retained Cash): } RM 5,000.00 - RM 750.00 = \mathbf{RM 4,250.00}$$ $$\text{Revenue Retention Rate: } \left( \frac{RM 4,250.00}{RM 5,000.00} \right) \times 100 = \mathbf{85.0\%}$$ Your store retained 85.0% of potential revenue as cash (15.0% discount loss). A retention rate of 85% - 95% is healthy. Below 80% indicates your promotions are giving away too much profit margin!

Metric: Average Order Value (AOV)

Measures the average ringgit amount spent by a customer during a single transaction.

$$\text{Average Order Value (RM)} = \frac{\text{Total Net Sales (RM)}}{\text{Total Transactions}}$$
Example:

If your store records a net revenue of RM 1,200 across 80 transactions in a day:
$$\text{Calculation: } RM 1,200 \div 80 = RM 15.00$$ Your AOV is RM 15.00. This tells you how much the average customer buys. Try to upsell pastries to increase this value!

Metric: Daily Average Revenue

Calculates the mean daily revenue, counting only active days where transactions took place.

$$\text{Daily Average Revenue (RM)} = \frac{\text{Total Net Sales (RM)}}{\text{Total Operating Days}}$$
Example:

If your total revenue is RM 9,000 over a month, and your store was open and running transactions for exactly 25 days (closed on Mondays):
$$\text{Calculation: } RM 9,000 \div 25 = RM 360.00$$ Your Daily Average is RM 360.00. This excludes closed days so you see a true operational daily target.

Metric: Payday Spend Drift Index (%)

Measures changes in customer spending during the Malaysian salary payout period (defined as the 25th to the 28th of each month).

$$\text{Spend Drift } (\%) = \left( \frac{\text{Payday Avg Spend} - \text{Standard Avg Spend}}{\text{Standard Avg Spend}} \right) \times 100$$
Example:

If customers spend an average of RM 18.00 per visit during payday dates (25th-28th) and only RM 15.00 on normal days:
$$\text{Calculation: } \left( \frac{RM 18.00 - RM 15.00}{RM 15.00} \right) \times 100 = +20.0\%$$ A drift of +20.0% means customers buy premium add-ons during payday week. Target high-margin seasonal menu promos in this window!

Metric: Promotion ROI Multiplier

Analyzes if marketing discounts are generating healthy revenues relative to the discount amount given away.

$$\text{ROI Multiplier} = \frac{\text{Gross Revenue} - \text{Total Discounts}}{\text{Total Discounts}} = \frac{\text{Net Revenue}}{\text{Total Discounts}}$$
Example:

If a "Buy 1 Free 1" promo generates RM 1,200 gross sales, but you gave away RM 200 in discounts:
$$\text{Net Revenue: } RM 1,200 - RM 200 = RM 1,000.00$$ $$\text{ROI Multiplier: } RM 1,000 \div RM 200 = 5.00x$$ An ROI of 5.0x is GOOD (Sustainable). An ROI score of 8.0x or more (≥ 8.0x) is Excellent, while < 5.0x suggests you are giving away too much discount!

Data Ingestion & ETL Cleanse

The gateway of the analytical database. The upload pipeline takes raw point-of-sale cashier spreadsheets, checks schema structures, flags missing recipes, and automatically fetches historical weather codes.

How to Use This Page (User Flow)
  1. Go to the Data Ingestion tab in the navigation menu.
  2. Ensure your POS system exports are saved in a standard 17-column CSV format.
  3. Drag and drop the CSV file into the upload dropzone, or click Browse Files to select it.
  4. Click Upload File. The loading indicators will display each verification phase.
  5. Once completed, review the Warehouse Profile panel on the right to verify total recorded entries, active categories, and total branches.
How It Works (Under the Hood Flow)

Executed automatically during ingestion, the system validates the 17 standard point-of-sale columns. It rejects the upload if duplicate transaction invoice numbers exist to prevent duplicate counting.

For every transaction date, the server calls the Open-Meteo Archive API (archive-api.open-meteo.com) using the branch coordinates. It aggregates weather codes during daylight hours (7:00 AM - 7:00 PM) to vote for a dominant weather condition label (Sunny, Cloudy, Raining, or Thunderstorm).

Calculations & Weather Logic
Metric: Unit Menu Price Extraction

Extracts the base menu cost per unit item, ignoring checkout promo modifiers.

$$\text{Unit Price} = \frac{\text{Gross Sales}}{\text{Quantity Sold}}$$
Example:

If an order lists 2 Hazelnut Lattes with a Gross_Sales total of RM 24.00:
$$\text{Calculation: } RM 24.00 \div 2 = RM 12.00$$ The system records the base menu price as RM 12.00 per unit. This allows the system to monitor price adjustments.

System Process: Automatic Data Cleaning & Layer Transformation

When a POS CSV is uploaded, raw cashier rows are automatically processed into structured analytical layers:

  1. Temporal Layer Breakdown: Converts raw Timestamp into transaction_date, transaction_time, Hour, Day Name, and Month for instant time-series analysis.
  2. Outlet Metadata Mapping: Matches Store_ID against the database branch registry to automatically attach store location names (e.g. Putrajaya, Puncak Alam), district, state, and geographic GPS coordinates.
  3. Automated Weather Enrichment: Queries historical Open-Meteo API for each branch location to tag hourly weather conditions (Sunny, Cloudy, Raining, Thunderstorm).
Rule Logic: Duplicate Prevention & Data Quality Safeguards

Protects analytical integrity by enforcing strict database validation rules during file ingestion:

  • Duplicate Receipt Protection: Skips or rejects transactions with existing Transaction_ID primary keys to prevent double-counting revenue and beverage volumes if the same CSV is uploaded twice.
  • Zero-Quantity & Void Filtering: Ignores test receipts, zero-quantity orders, or invalid cashier entries to ensure accurate Average Order Value (AOV) calculations.
  • Dynamic Product ID Registry: Automatically assigns internal product identifiers (e.g., SKU-1042) to newly introduced menu items.
Rule Logic: Weather Ingestion Voting

How weather data is mapped into the database when you upload a POS CSV:

Condition Label Meteorological API Metric Check Impact on Sales
Sunny / Fair Precipitation less than 2.5mm and clear sky codes are the majority from 07:00 to 19:00 ☀️ Baseline / Slight sales boost
Cloudy Precipitation less than 2.5mm and cloud cover codes are the majority from 07:00 to 19:00 ☁️ Baseline trend (Normal traffic)
Raining Precipitation is 2.5mm or more (and no thunderstorm codes are registered) 🌧️ 30% traffic drop on average
Thunderstorm Precipitation is 2.5mm or more and severe thunderstorm codes are registered ⛈️ 60% traffic drop on average
Example:

If you upload sales for July 12th, the system calls historical records. If total rain was 5mm during the day, it labels that day as Raining. The AI uses this label to learn how much rain reduces your revenue.

AI Sales Forecast & Ingredient Planner

The predictive core of the platform. It generates a 5-day sales plan using Facebook Prophet, combines it with real-time weather forecasts, and translates revenue numbers into an exact ingredient shopping guide.

How to Use This Page (User Flow)
  1. Navigate to the AI Forecast in the sidebar.
  2. Choose your target outlet from the Select Branch dropdown.
  3. Click Generate Forecast. Watch the 5-step pipeline fetch live weather and run models.
  4. Inspect the Expected 5-Day Revenue and Forecast Accuracy dials.
  5. Review the Ingredient Shopping Guide to see exactly how much coffee beans, milk, chocolate, and ice to purchase.
  6. Download a print-ready shopping list by clicking Download PDF at the top right.
How It Works (Under the Hood Flow)

The forecasting engine pulls past sales data. It calls the OpenWeatherMap Forecast API (api.openweathermap.org) to retrieve future 5-day forecasts in 3-hour slots, filtering down to business hours (9:00 AM - 9:00 PM) to calculate daily weather conditions and average temperatures.

It trains a Facebook Prophet multiplicative regression model using five regressors: weather forecast labels, promotions, weekdays, weekends, and public holidays. Expected beverage units are calculated from historical sales ratios, which are then scaled by recipe ingredient weights.

Forecasting Formulas & Calculations
Formula: Prophet Multiplicative Regression Model

Calculates sales predictions by dynamically scaling a baseline revenue trend up or down based on real-world factors (weekly seasonality, public holidays, and live weather forecasts).

$$y(t) = g(t) \times \Big(1 + s(t) + h(t) + w(t)\Big) + \epsilon_t$$

Parameter Definitions:
• \(y(t)\) = Final Projected Revenue (RM) for day \(t\)
• \(g(t)\) = Baseline Growth Trend (core daily sales level without temporary factors)
• \(s(t)\) = Weekly Seasonality factor (e.g. \(+0.15\) for Saturday weekend boost)
• \(h(t)\) = Public Holiday & Campaign factor (e.g. \(+0.20\) for festive promos)
• \(w(t)\) = Forecasted Weather Effect (e.g. \(-0.40\) for heavy rain drag)
• \(\epsilon_t\) = Margin of Error (residual noise; assumed \(0\) for expected point prediction)

Step-by-Step Calculation Example:

If Putrajaya outlet has a baseline revenue trend \(g(t) = \mathbf{\text{RM 1,000.00}}\), and tomorrow is a Saturday (+15%) during a Public Holiday Campaign (+20%), but Heavy Rain / Thunderstorm (-40%) is forecasted:

1. Combined Multiplier: \(1 + s(t) + h(t) + w(t) = 1 + (+0.15) + (+0.20) + (-0.40) = 0.95\)
2. Final Forecasted Revenue: \(y(t) = \text{RM 1,000.00} \times 0.95 = \mathbf{\text{RM 950.00}}\)

The system adjusts the final prediction to RM 950.00 because heavy rain reduces walk-in customers despite weekend and holiday demand.

Metric: Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE)

Measures model accuracy by comparing past predictions to what was actually earned.

$$\text{Average Prediction Error (\%)} = \frac{100\%}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \left| \frac{\text{Actual Sales}_i - \text{Predicted Sales}_i}{\text{Actual Sales}_i} \right|$$
Example:

If you predicted RM 500 but made RM 450 (10% error) on Day 1, and predicted RM 300 but made RM 330 (10% error) on Day 2:
$$\text{Average Error: } (10\% + 10\%) \div 2 = 10\%$$ Your MAPE is 10%. This gives you a Forecast Accuracy of 90% (100% - 10%). A higher accuracy means more reliable purchasing schedules!

Formula: Stock Demand Conversion

Calculates the total weight of ingredients required to satisfy the forecasted cup sales.

$$\text{Total Ingredient Required} = \sum \Big( \text{Predicted Sales (Cups)} \times \text{Ingredient Weight per Cup} \Big)$$
Example:

If the system forecasts sales of 150 Hot Lattes and 80 Iced Lattes for the next 5 days, it looks up the latte recipe weights to calculate the shopping list:

Ingredient Recipe Weight per Cup Calculation Process Total Order Volume
Coffee Beans 18.0 g (150 cups × 18 g) + (80 cups × 18 g) = 4,140 g 4.14 kg of beans
Fresh Milk 200.0 ml (150 cups × 200 ml) + (80 cups × 200 ml) = 46,000 ml 46.0 Liters of milk
Rule Logic: New Item & Missing Recipe Audit

How the system handles newly introduced menu items uploaded from POS transaction files:

  1. Automatic Detection: When a POS CSV contains a new drink or food item not yet registered in the database, the system flags it with a Missing Recipe status.
  2. Pre-Forecast Alert: On the AI Forecast page, an alert banner (⚠️ Missing Recipes Detected) notifies the owner to define ingredient weights before generating inventory plans.
  3. Recipe Definition: The owner clicks Define Recipes Now to enter raw material weights per cup serving (e.g. 18g coffee beans, 200ml milk, 15ml syrup).
  4. Procurement Sync: Once saved, the forecast engine instantly incorporates the new item into the 5-day Ingredient Procurement & Shopping Guide.

Report Audit Calculations
Calculation: Forecast Difference (%)

Calculates the percentage variance between actual daily revenue and predicted values.

$$\text{Forecast Variance (\%)} = \left( \frac{\text{Actual Sales (RM)} - \text{Predicted Sales (RM)}}{\text{Predicted Sales (RM)}} \right) \times 100$$
Example:

If you predicted RM 500.00 but actually made RM 550.00:
$$\text{Calculation: } \left( \frac{RM 550 - RM 500}{RM 500} \right) \times 100 = +10.0\%$$ A variance of +10.0% falls inside the Safe Range badge (between -15% and +15%). If the variance is within ±5%, the report highlights it as On Target!

Rule Logic: Diagnostic Badges

The report evaluates daily accuracy using these rules:

Diagnostic Badge Variance Rule Meaning
On Target Within ±5% of prediction 🟢 Highly accurate prediction
Safe Range Within ±15% of prediction 🔵 Acceptable predicting variance
Off Range Exceeds ±15% variance 🔴 Prediction was significantly off
Exceeded Actual sales exceed predictions by more than 15% ⚡ Sales surged beyond baseline expectations

Holiday Auto-Tune Calculations
Formula: Holiday Multiplier / Holiday Effect

Calculates the relative difference in sales during public holidays compared to normal operational days.

$$\text{Holiday Sales Variance} = \frac{\text{Average Holiday Sales (RM)} - \text{Average Normal Sales (RM)}}{\text{Average Normal Sales (RM)}}$$
Example:

If your Putrajaya office branch averages RM 300.00 on normal weekdays, but drops to RM 195.00 on public holidays when offices close:
$$\text{Calculation: } \frac{RM 195 - RM 300}{RM 300} = -0.35$$ The learned holiday multiplier is -0.35 (or a -35% drop). The AI automatically applies this drop to holiday forecasts for this branch!

4. Multi-Outlet Behavior & Ramadhan Core Profiles

Our predictive analytics models adapt automatically to distinct outlet behaviors and localized demographic trends.

Putrajaya Stall (STB-PJ1)

Target Profile: Corporate Weekday Traffic.
Operational Pattern: High volume from Monday to Friday, catering to office workers. Dips heavily during public holidays as commuters migrate away.

Puncak Alam (FT-PA1)

Target Profile: Residential Weekend Leisure.
Operational Pattern: Peak sales occur on Saturdays and Sundays. Experiences festive lifts during local school and national holidays.

Ramadhan Mode

Target Profile: Evening Break-Fast Surge.
Behavior Rule: Normal afternoon coffee traffic drops close to zero. Peak demand shifts to evening break-fast hours (18:00 - 20:00).
